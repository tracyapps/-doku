Result display · five encodings

*doku results

One result, five ways to show it. Every version carries the same four dimensions — active time, first-entry accuracy, hints, and answer checks — plus the difficulty it was played at. Each one collapses into a small tile that can sit next to a friend’s, and all of them read their colors from the player’s active skin.

The shared result grammar

Whatever the layout, these five atoms mean the same thing. Difficulty is always spelled out — never color alone — and assistance is always a separate seal, never folded into accuracy.

00 — GRAMMAR

Difficulty

Three named steps, easy to hard. It stamps the result and sets the only fair basis for comparing speed.

First-entry accuracy

The ring fills with the percentage of your first pen entries that were right. Givens, notes, erasures and revealed squares are excluded.

Hints & checks

Counted independently as pips plus a number. Lower is calmer, but it is never a penalty to your accuracy.

Assistance seal

Solid means unassisted. Outlined means a hint, reveal, check or note-autofill was used — recorded even if it was turned off before the end.

Context line

sudoku level easy Sep 26

Variant, level, and date travel with every result so a slow, clean solve is never read as the same thing as a fast, assisted one.

Run — your board, as the chart

The most distinctive bet: the result is an 81-cell fingerprint of how the puzzle was solved. Givens, first-entry hits, hints and reveals each get their own mark, so the shape of the solve is visible, not just its totals.

Radar — your shape against theirs

Four axes, one silhouette. Speed, accuracy, independence and focus form a polygon you can overlay on a friend’s in a glance. The three guide rings are the difficulty levels; the ring matching this puzzle is drawn heavier.

Reads as: personality. Two players with the same time get visibly different shapes. The speed axis only appears when there is more than one result to scale against — a lone solve has no reference time.

Tracks — everyone on the same scale

The comparison-native layout. Each dimension is a track; every player is a marker normalised so that better is always to the right. Ties are allowed, and no single number decides who “won.”

Tape — the shareable line

The Wordle successor: a fixed-width, spoiler-free line that survives being pasted into any chat. Two lines, four real dimensions, comparable at a glance — and it still works when the recipient is on a different theme.

Reads as: a claim you can drop in a group chat. No grid to decode, no answer leaked, and every line lines up because it is monospaced.

Grid — the scorecard

A matrix of players against dimensions. Fill intensity shows relative quality within each row, a star marks the row best, and ties produce more than one star. Achievements are named underneath so several people can leave a winner.

How these behave

NOTES

Theme-proof by construction

Every surface reads the game’s own tokens — --bg, --surface, --text, --muted, --accent, --grid-line. Switch the skin control at the top; nothing is hard-coded to one theme.

No invented scores

Tracks and Radar normalise only within the results actually on screen, and say so. There is no fabricated “par” time and no hidden weighted total. If a combined index is ever added, its formula belongs on the card.

Difficulty stays honest

Speed is only comparable inside a level, so every layout labels the level and never implies that an easy solve equals a hard one. Achievements are recognised across levels instead of forcing a false equivalence.

Accessibility is part of the design

Color is never the only channel: difficulty is text plus steps, counts are always printed as numbers, and each chart exposes a text alternative. Motion respects reduced-motion, and every control is a native, keyboard-focusable input.
